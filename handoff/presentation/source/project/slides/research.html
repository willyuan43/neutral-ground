Research and iteration

What we read, and what players taught us

Research gathered

  • UNCCD Land Degradation Neutrality: avoid, reduce, reverse, within the same kind of land
  • SF Recreation and Park’s 2006 natural areas plan for Glen Canyon’s grassland
  • NOAA records: the 2021 drought year and the New Year’s Eve 2022 storm
  • Youth climate survey (Lancet, 2021) and how hit games teach a first minute

What playtests changed

  • Three independent review rounds scored 5, 6 and 6.5 out of 10
  • “Looks like Google Maps, feels like a PowerPoint”: a toy diorama, 3D characters and a story start
  • “Where is this, and what do I do?”: your own character, three stars, a task list and an arrow
  • “One click skipped a year”: a year starts only when your tasks are done
We read UNCCD's Land Degradation Neutrality framework, the city's 2006 natural-areas plan for this park, and NOAA's weather records. Then we tested hard. Outside reviews and real first-time players were blunt: it looked like a map and felt like slides. Each round changed the game: the toy look, real 3D characters, a playable character, clear goals, and no accidental year skips.
